Workshops 8, 4 and 11

Transient flows

Three animations of time-dependent simulations.

Behind the cylinder, vortices peel off alternately from the top and bottom and travel downstream. The Y-velocity contours show alternating bands of increasing and decreasing velocity, which change the pressure distribution around the cylinder. This produces a cyclic force on the cylinder, which could cause destructive resonance if its frequency matched the natural frequency of the structure.

The stirrer sheds vorticity from its blade tips, with the rotating region meshed separately from the stationary portion. The inkjet is a multiphase flow where the jet thins and breaks into a main droplet.

Workshop 6

Heat exchanger

Cool fluid flowing across heated tubes. Only a thin layer of fluid next to each tube is heated directly and warm wakes trail downstream behind each tube.

The model covers a small section of the exchanger with a symmetry line through the middle to keep the cell count down

Tube walls held at 450 K heat incoming fluid at about 275 K. The steepest temperature gradients sit on the front face of each tube, where the boundary layer is thinnest.

Workshop 12

Slurry transport through pipe bends

Alumina slurry flowing through a pipe with two 90° bends, solved as a multiphase flow. In the horizontal runs the heavier solids settle towards the bottom wall, and each bend throws them back towards the outside of the turn.

Similair to the multiphase flow I explore in my thesis studies.

At the outlet, the alumina volume fraction ranges from about 4% at the top of the pipe to 19% at the bottom, around a mean of 10%.

Workshop 2

Developing turbulent pipe flow

Water entering a 100mm pipe with a flat velocity profile at 3 m/s. The wall boundary slows the outer flow, so the centre speeds up until the profile ressembles a that flow is fully developed.

The pipe is modelled in two stages. The first model covers the first 25 diameters from a uniform inlet. Its outlet velocity and turbulence profiles then become the inlet of a second model, which covers 25 to 50 diameters, so together they show how far down the pipe the flow takes to settle.

Centreline velocity rises from 3.0 to about 3.55 m/s over the first 25 diameters. The pressure drop over that stretch is about 1,760 Pa, falling to about 1,510 Pa over the next 25 diameters once the effects of entrance loss have diminished.

Workshop 1

Mixing elbow

A side branch injects a fast jet into the bend of a larger pipe. Steady 3D flow with the k-ε turbulence model, inflation layers at the walls and residuals solved to 10⁻⁶.

Workshop 3

Turbulent free jet

A jet discharging into still surroundings. Focus here was the bodies of influence to concentrate mesh refinement in regions of interest. The tetrahedral mesh was also then converted to polyhedral cells.

Workshop 5

Orifice plate

Turbulent flow through a sharp-edged orifice that is intended to represent a common flow meter. Run on a coarse and a fine mesh with refinement in the momentum source region.
